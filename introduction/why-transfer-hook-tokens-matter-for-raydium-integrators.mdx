A Transfer Hook puts a token's rules on the mint itself, so a website or an off-chain agreement no longer has to enforce them. Token-2022 runs the rule on every transfer, including the transfers inside a Raydium swap. This page explains what that lets a Raydium integrator build and why Raydium needs changes to support it.

## What integrators can build

A launchpad, issuer, protocol, or trading app can use a hook to enforce rules like these.

- Limit early buys, account balances, or buys per slot during a launch.
- Pay holders a reward based on both balance and how long they held it.
- Stop a creator's dedicated account from falling below a public vesting schedule.
- Allow transfers only to accounts that meet an eligibility rule.

The repository includes three example hooks that show the first three.

| Example | Who wants it | What it does | What it does not do |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fair Launch | A launch team or a community | Caps what one buy, one account, and one slot can take. With only the per-slot budget on, it limits bundled buys | Stop one person from using many accounts, stop buys spread across slots, or see a tip paid to a block builder |
| Creator Commitment | A project that wants a creator's allocation to vest in public | The dedicated account cannot fall below the unvested amount, and moving tokens to another wallet does not get around it | Prove the creator holds no other tokens, or stop a burn |
| Holder Rewards | A project paying holders, or spinning a child token off a parent | Tracks balance multiplied by time held and settles inside the transfer. A one-time mode funds the allocation once | Pay holders who never register, create the child token, or collect fees by itself |

The rule stays with the Token-2022 mint, so it applies in every app that supports the hook, not just one frontend.

## Why Raydium needs changes

A Raydium swap contains two token transfers, one into the pool and one out. For a hooked mint, Token-2022 calls the hook on each transfer, and the hook may need extra accounts such as a config, a counter, or a holder record. Raydium has to receive those accounts and forward them to the correct Token-2022 transfer. The original Raydium instructions do not forward them, so they reject hooked mints.

This project adds hook-aware instructions to forks of the Raydium programs. The original instructions stay unchanged for ordinary tokens.

| AMM | Hook-aware instructions |
| --- | --- |
| CPMM | `swap_base_input_v2`, plus `_v2` versions of the exact-output swap, pool creation, deposit, withdraw, permissioned pool creation, and protocol, fund, and creator fee collection |
| CLMM | `swap_v3`, plus `open_position_v3`, `increase_liquidity_v3`, `decrease_liquidity_v3`, limit order, reward, and fee collection instructions |

Any hook can run through these instructions. Raydium does not have to approve it, and the SDK and instruction builders need no changes for a new hook.

## Who pays

| Cost | Who pays | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deploying the hook program | Whoever deploys it | Refundable rent of about 5.1 SOL per MB. The example programs are 126 to 169 KB, about 0.7 to 0.9 SOL each |
| Per-mint setup | The mint's hook authority | Rent for the validation list and config. About 0.005 SOL per mint for Fair Launch, by the rent formula |
| Compute for each hooked transfer | The trader, as part of the swap | About 60,000 to 65,000 compute units for a CPMM swap with one hooked leg and no extra accounts |
| Approving a hooked mint for pool creation | Whoever runs the Raydium deployment | A transaction fee and the rent for the approval record |

<Warning>
  Raydium CPMM and CLMM accept a Token-2022 mint with a TransferHook extension into a new pool only after the program admin approves that mint. This is Raydium's existing rule, and the approval is per mint, not per hook program. Whoever deploys the hook-aware forks holds the admin key and runs `raydium-hook mint approve`.
</Warning>
